## Daily, Weekly, Yearly: What the Standards Say.

Use it to decide which tasks you want and how often. Summers quotes the visits you choose.

### How often should an office be cleaned?

As often as its traffic needs. No federal rule sets one schedule: OSHA requires a workplace kept clean as far as the work allows, with food-waste bins emptied each working day they are used. The CDC says to clean touched surfaces regularly, more often where traffic is high, and the carpet industry calls for daily vacuuming in busy areas.

- **Every working day**: OSHA and the Carpet and Rug Institute. Empty the bins for food waste in eating areas on every working day they are used, under OSHA's sanitation standard (29 CFR 1910.141). Vacuum entrances, reception areas, lobbies and corridors: the Carpet and Rug Institute's commercial carpet standard, CRI 204, puts them on a daily schedule.
- **Regularly, more often when busy**: CDC. Wipe the surfaces people touch: desks, counters, door handles, touchpads, stair rails, elevator buttons and restroom fixtures. The CDC suggests cleaning high-traffic spaces more often.
- **Two or three times a week**: Carpet and Rug Institute. Vacuum private offices, meeting rooms and boardrooms. CRI 204 counts them as less-used areas that can go on this schedule.
- **Through the year, by traffic**: Carpet and Rug Institute. A low-moisture interim carpet clean, which can put off a deep clean, and a deep clean: each once or twice a year where traffic is moderate. Heavy traffic: 6 to 12 interim and 2 to 6 deep cleans a year. Severe: 12 to 52 and 2 to 26, with soil-prone spots deep cleaned weekly.
- **When it looks dirty**: CDC. Every other surface, the CDC says, gets cleaned when it is visibly dirty.
- **All the time**: OSHA. The baseline: every workplace kept clean as far as the nature of the work allows, and workroom floors kept dry where practicable.

3. **List the tasks for each visit**: Trash, restrooms, touched surfaces and floors. Write down what should happen every visit and what can wait for a deeper one.

## Questions About Office Cleaning Schedules.

### Do restrooms need their own cleaning schedule?

No federal rule sets a number. The CDC lists restroom fixtures among the high-touch surfaces to clean regularly, and more often where traffic is high, and OSHA requires washing facilities to be kept in a sanitary condition. Base the schedule on how many people use them each day.

### Does OSHA say how often an office must be cleaned?

Not with a set schedule. OSHA's sanitation standard, 29 CFR 1910.141, says workplaces must be kept clean to the extent the nature of the work allows. Its one daily rule is for eating areas: bins for food waste must be emptied at least once each working day unless unused.
